What this is

MurrValley Histories is a research collection. For each subject it gathers the sources we could reach and pairs them with narratives written to give further research a running start: cited histories, timelines and people pages. Every fact is footnoted to where it came from, so each narrative is also a map of the sources behind it.

These are not definitive histories. They are built from material available online or through library-card databases: newspapers from the 1890s on, photographs and records in the Lucretia Little History Room at the Mill Valley Public Library, and websites. Much is still out of reach. The Marin Independent Journal for 1979 to 2001 survives mostly on microfilm and has not been read, interviews with the people who ran the clubs and teams are still to come, and some archives have not been seen. Where sources disagree, the pages say so.

Use it the way you would use a good bibliography. Each site has a Sources page listing every item, with links where a free copy exists, and Research guides that explain how to see the rest at the libraries that hold it. Follow a footnote to the original, read it in full, and check it before you quote or publish.

How it was made. The research and first drafts were done by AI research assistants (Claude, made by Anthropic), working under the direction of the sites’ editor, Dan Murray, who chose the subjects, set the rules and reviewed the work. Every claim was checked against the text of its source before it was published.

Storyteller. The Mill Valley Storyteller searches all three collections at once, including the many articles and records not yet used in any history, and writes a short account with numbered citations. Access is by individual account, approved by Dan; each person gets 10 Quick searches and 5 Deep research searches a week. Its answers are leads to follow, not finished history.

Corrections and additions are welcome through Contact us on any of the sites.
